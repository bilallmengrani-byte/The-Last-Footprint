# The Last Footprint
### Abandoned but Not Forgotten

*Every mission leaves something behind.*

The Last Footprint is an interactive 3D space archive about the spacecraft and robotic explorers that remain on the Moon, Mars, and beyond long after their missions have ended.

It brings together the stories of these machines, what they were built to do, and the mark they left on space exploration.

## About the Project

We often remember space missions for their launches, discoveries, and achievements. But not much attention is given to what happens to the machines once those missions are over.

Some are still sitting on the lunar surface. Others remain on Mars, while a few continue travelling through deep space.

The Last Footprint gives these machines a place in the spotlight through an interactive experience where visitors can explore their stories and learn about their missions.

## Featured Missions

The archive currently features six missions and spacecraft:

- **Apollo 11 Lunar Module Descent Stage** — The part of the Lunar Module that remained on the Moon after the first crewed lunar landing in 1969.
- **Apollo 15 Lunar Roving Vehicle (LRV-1)** — The first crewed lunar rover, used by Apollo 15 astronauts to travel across and explore the Moon.
- **Sojourner Rover** — NASA's first Mars rover, which arrived with the Mars Pathfinder mission and helped demonstrate the possibilities of robotic exploration.
- **Opportunity Rover** — A Mars rover that operated far beyond its original planned mission and made important discoveries about the planet.
- **Ingenuity Mars Helicopter** — The experimental helicopter that made the first powered, controlled flights on another planet.
- **Pioneer Spacecraft** — The pioneering deep-space missions that travelled beyond the inner Solar System and helped us learn more about the planets and space beyond them.

Each mission has its own story, from the first steps on the Moon to robotic exploration on Mars and journeys into deep space.

## Exploring the Archive

Inside the project, visitors can:

- Explore 3D environments of Earth, the Moon, and Mars.
- Switch between planetary environments.
- Discover spacecraft and missions associated with each location.
- Read about the history, purpose, and achievements of each mission.
- Explore space through an interactive visual experience.

## Built With

The project uses a combination of web technologies and 3D tools:

- **React 19 & TypeScript** — Interface and application structure.
- **Vite** — Development and production builds.
- **Three.js** — 3D graphics and environments.
- **React Three Fiber & Drei** — 3D scenes and controls.
- **GSAP** — Camera animations and cinematic transitions.
- **Tailwind CSS** — Styling and layout.
- **Custom GLSL shaders** — Atmospheric effects and visual details.
- **Lucide** — Interface icons.

## Why The Last Footprint?

A mission doesn't lose its importance just because it has ended.

The machines left behind on distant worlds represent years of engineering, exploration, and human curiosity. They may no longer be active, but they remain part of the history of how we explored space.

**Their missions ended. Their stories didn't.**
